Guest Categories and Policies

Guest age categories and hotel policies decide who counts toward a room's capacity, how extras are priced, and what Brixa tells guests about cancellations and rules. Getting these right keeps quotes accurate and prevents awkward corrections later.

Guest categories and age bands

Brixa groups the people in a booking into categories your hotel defines. A typical setup looks like this:

CategoryTypical age rangeNotes
Adults13 and overAlways enabled; open-ended at the top.
Children3–12Age range adjustable to match your pricing.
Infants0–2Often stay free or use a crib.
Teenagers / young adults16–18Optional category for hotels that price teens separately.
Pets—No age range; enable if you accept pets.

The age ranges are yours to adjust, with one rule: they must connect without gaps, so every age from newborn to adult falls into exactly one category. When a guest says "two adults and a 10-year-old", Brixa uses your ranges to classify the child correctly.

How categories affect quotes

  • Occupancy: each room type has separate limits per category (for example, up to 2 adults and 2 children). Brixa only offers rooms that genuinely fit the party.
  • Pricing of extras: products can apply to specific categories — breakfast might be paid for adults but free for infants. Category ranges therefore change the quote total.
  • Clarifying questions: when a guest mentions ages, Brixa maps them to your categories; if the party does not fit a single room, it proposes alternatives instead of overbooking a room.

If you change an age range, review your per-category product prices and room occupancy limits at the same time, so all three stay consistent. Quotes built from these settings are covered in Creating and Sending Quotes.

Cancellation and other policies

Each hotel maintains its own set of policies, and each option in a quote carries a cancellation policy. For every policy, provide:

  1. A clear name guests will recognize (for example, "Flexible" or "Non-refundable").
  2. A plain-language description of the terms — deadlines, charges, exceptions. Brixa uses this text to answer questions like "Can I cancel for free?".
  3. A link to the full policy document, if you have one, so guests can read the complete terms.

Brixa communicates the policy attached to the option a guest is considering, so keep descriptions honest and unambiguous — vague wording leads to disputes.

Staying in sync with your PMS

If your hotel has a connected PMS (such as Mews), treat it as the source of truth and mirror any policy or category changes in Brixa promptly. A mismatch — for example, a stricter cancellation deadline in your PMS than in Brixa — means guests get told one thing and charged another. After changes, send a test question about the policy to confirm Brixa's answer matches what your PMS enforces.
