Handling Cases That Need You

When Brixa asks for help, it's either an escalation that pauses the conversation and waits for your team, or a review flag that lets Brixa keep replying while asking for a second look. This article walks through finding both, understanding what happened, and resolving them cleanly.

Finding conversations that need attention

  1. Sign in at app.brixa.ai and open your conversation list.
  2. Escalated conversations show a shield icon — red for urgent cases, amber for standard ones. Conversations with a review flag instead show a muted flag icon. Hovering over either shows why it was flagged.
  3. Use the Escalated filter to see escalations and review flags together, with the most urgent and oldest cases first.

You'll also receive an email notification when a conversation is escalated or flagged for review, so nothing depends on someone watching the inbox.

Reviewing what happened

For an escalation, the banner inside the conversation reads "Manual Review Required" and the composer carries the same tag while it's paused. For a review flag, a neutral banner reads "Flagged for review" instead — nothing is paused, and Brixa keeps replying while you look into it. Either banner tells you in plain language why it appeared, a short hint on what to do, and when it was flagged. On an escalation, the technical details support may ask for sit behind the wrench icon on that banner. Before acting, get the full picture:

  • Read the guest's recent messages to understand what they actually want.
  • Check what Brixa already replied or drafted. In some cases you'll find a suggested reply waiting that you can edit and send.
  • Review the booking details and any quote in the conversation, so your answer matches what the guest was offered.

Resolving an escalation

What resolution looks like depends on the reason for the escalation:

  • Reply to the guest. Write your own message or start from Brixa's draft. Sending manually always works, even while the conversation is escalated.
  • Decide on a change. For modifications to a booking that already has a hold in place — Tentatively Booked or Confirmed by Hotel — accept or decline the change in the conversation. Either way, review the reply to the guest before it goes out so it says what you intend.
  • Fix the underlying issue. If the escalation came from missing or wrong information — booking details, availability, a PMS problem — correct it first so the same issue doesn't recur.

When the case is handled, select Resume in the conversation. Brixa takes the conversation back, and paused follow-ups are re-evaluated so the guest isn't forgotten.

Handling a review flag

A review flag doesn't stop anything — Brixa keeps replying to the guest while you look into it. Open the conversation to see what prompted the flag: an attachment Brixa couldn't read, a reply it wasn't fully confident about, an ambiguous request, or a possible VIP or high-value guest.

  • Nothing to do. If the flag doesn't need action — the conversation moved on, or the attachment turned out not to matter — select Dismiss on the banner.
  • Needs a closer look. If you decide the conversation should be handled by a person, select Escalate. This turns the review flag into a full escalation, pausing automation until someone resumes it.

Some review flags clear themselves once the conversation moves on cleanly — an ambiguous request that gets answered, for example. Others, like an attachment nobody has opened, stay until someone dismisses them.

Escalating a conversation yourself

You can also put any conversation into human hands manually — useful when you're negotiating something special or you know a guest needs personal treatment. Add a short reason so colleagues understand why automation is paused. This has the same effect as selecting Escalate on a review flag: automation pauses until someone resumes it.

Good habits

  • Respond to urgent escalations within minutes — the guest is usually waiting.
  • Before resuming automation, make sure the guest's last message has an answer and the booking details are correct, so Brixa continues from a clean state.
  • Don't let review flags pile up — dismiss the ones you've already looked at so the flag icon keeps meaning something.
  • Don't leave conversations escalated indefinitely; while escalated, Brixa won't reply or follow up automatically. If a conversation truly can't continue, see When a Conversation Can't Continue Automatically.
