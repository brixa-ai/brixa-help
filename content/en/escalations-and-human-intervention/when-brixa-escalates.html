When Brixa Asks for Human Help

Brixa handles most guest messages on its own, but sometimes it needs a person. There are three ways it asks: an escalation, which pauses the conversation and waits for your team; a held reply, which keeps the conversation automated but holds one reply back for your team to review; and a review flag, which means a reply has already gone out and Brixa just wants someone to take a look. Here is where each one appears and what to do.

Escalations: Brixa pauses and waits

Brixa escalates a conversation when it needs a decision only your team can make — an upset guest, a special request, a change to a booking that already has a hold in place.

  • In your inbox at app.brixa.ai. Escalated conversations show a shield icon — a red shield for an urgent escalation, an amber shield for a standard one. Hover over it to see why the conversation was flagged.
  • By email. Your team receives an email notification when a conversation is escalated, so nothing depends on someone happening to be watching the inbox.
  • Inside the conversation. A banner reads "Manual Review Required" and tells you in plain language what happened, what to do next, and when it was flagged. The composer carries the same tag for as long as the conversation is paused. If Brixa support asks for the technical details behind an escalation, click the wrench icon on the banner: it reveals the escalation codes, internal labels and context, with a Copy button you can paste into your support message.

Use the Escalated filter to see everything that needs a look in one list, most urgent first. A red shield means a guest is likely waiting right now — for example, they asked to speak with a person; open these first and aim to respond within minutes. An amber shield means the conversation needs a decision or a review but is less time-critical.

An escalated conversation stays in human hands until someone on your team hands it back. During that time, Brixa does not send anything to the guest automatically and scheduled follow-ups are paused. You may still find a suggested reply waiting for you to edit and send, and you can always reply yourself.

Held replies: Brixa keeps going, but holds one reply for you

Brixa can hold a single reply back so your team can read it before the guest sees anything. Instead of pausing the whole conversation, only that reply waits for you; everything else stays automated.

  • In your inbox at app.brixa.ai. Conversations with a held reply show an amber flag icon, separate from the shield used for escalations. Hover over it to see why the reply was held.
  • By email. Your team gets notified so someone can review the reply.
  • Inside the conversation. An amber banner reads "Held for review" and shows when the reply was held. The banner shows a P2 badge. You can reply to the guest yourself, dismiss the hold, or escalate if the conversation needs a person.

A held reply clears on its own once Brixa answers the guest on a later turn, so if the guest writes again and Brixa replies, you don't need to dismiss anything. If you've already dealt with it, you can also dismiss it yourself.

If Brixa could not process a message at all, you get an amber flag asking you to reply yourself; the guest is told the team is looking into it.

Review flags: the reply went out, but Brixa wants a second look

Some situations deserve a person's eyes after the fact. Brixa has already replied to the guest and keeps replying as normal, but it flags the conversation so someone can check the reply when convenient.

  • In your inbox at app.brixa.ai. Conversations with a review flag show a grey flag icon, separate from the amber flag used for held replies and the shield used for escalations. Hover over it to see why the conversation was flagged.
  • By email. Your team gets notified so someone can take a look when convenient — there's no urgency the way there is with an escalation.
  • Inside the conversation. A grey banner reads "Flagged — take a look" and shows when the flag was raised. Nothing is paused or held: the reply has already been sent, Brixa keeps sending replies to the guest while the flag is up, and there's no composer tag.

From the banner you can:

  • Dismiss the flag once you've seen it and there's nothing more to do.
  • Escalate the conversation if, after looking, you decide it needs a person to take over. This turns it into a full escalation and pauses automation the same way as above.

A review flag stays until someone dismisses it, even if the conversation moves on. The Escalated filter in your conversation list includes escalations, held replies and review flags, so you can see everything that needs a look in one place.

Payment and availability alerts

Two alerts concern a guest's guarantee or payment. Each banner tells you what happened and what to do:

  • Could not check the room before taking the guarantee. Brixa could not confirm the room was still available when the guest opened the pay link. Check your PMS, then click Request guarantee, or ask the guest to open the link again.
  • Payment received — create the booking in your PMS. The guest paid and there is no reservation in your PMS yet. Create the booking in your PMS yourself, then confirm the quote. If the room is gone, the refund is your decision, made in your payment gateway.

What to do

  1. For an escalation: open the conversation, read the banner and the guest's recent messages, handle the situation, then resume automation.
  2. For a held reply: hover over the amber flag to see why the reply was held, open the conversation and read the guest's recent messages, then send your own reply, dismiss the hold, or escalate if the conversation needs a person. If you do nothing and Brixa answers the guest on a later turn, the hold clears on its own.
  3. For a review flag: hover over the grey flag to see why it was raised, open the conversation and read the reply that already went out, then dismiss the flag or escalate if it turns out to need a takeover.
